BODMAS & Calculation Order

Round 1 & Round 2 Syllabus • Bracket Priorities • The 'Of' Operator
▼
The Golden Rule: Mathematical expressions must never be solved randomly left-to-right. When operators compete, the standard priority hierarchy dictates which operation executes first.
Hierarchical Precedence Pyramid
Essential Rules
Nested Bracket Evaluation Order
( ) ⟶ { } ⟶ [ ]
Always resolve the innermost parentheses ( ) first, then curly braces { }, and lastly outer square brackets [ ].
The 'Of' Operator Precedence
A ÷ B of C = A ÷ (B × C)
Critical Trap: 'Of' is evaluated before regular division or multiplication. For example: 60 ÷ 5 of 3 = 60 ÷ 15 = 4 (NOT 60 ÷ 5 × 3 = 36).
💡 Worked Test Problem
Evaluate: 84 - 7 × [15 - (9 - 3 of 2)] ÷ 6
Step 1: 'Of' first: 3 of 2 = 6.
Step 2: Innermost bracket: 9 - 6 = 3.
Step 3: Square brackets: 15 - 3 = 12.
Step 4: Multiply & divide left-to-right: 7 × 12 ÷ 6 = 84 ÷ 6 = 14.
Step 5: Subtraction: 84 - 14 = 70. (Answer: 70)
⚠️ Common Student Mistake
Do not confuse 3/4 ÷ 3/4 of 3/4. The 'of' joins the second fraction into a single denominator: 3/4 ÷ (9/16) = 3/4 × 16/9 = 4/3.

Quadratic Equations & Roots

Round 1 & Round 4 Syllabus • Discriminant • Vieta's Relations
▼
Standard Quadratic Form: ax² + bx + c = 0 (where a ≠ 0). The value of the discriminant determines how many real solutions exist and what the parabola looks like on a graph.
Discriminant & Parabola X-Axis Intersections
Quadratic Formulas & Discriminant Table
The Quadratic Formula & Discriminant D
x = (-b ± √D) / (2a) where D = b² - 4ac
• D > 0: Two distinct real roots.
• D = 0: Two real and equal roots: x = -b / (2a).
• D < 0: No real roots (two complex conjugate roots).
• If a, b, c are rational and D is a perfect square, roots are rational; if D is not a perfect square, roots are conjugate surds.
Vieta's Formulas (Sum & Product of Roots)
Sum: α + β = -b/a | Product: α × β = c/a
• Forming Equation from Roots: x² - (Sum)x + (Product) = 0
• Sum of Squares: α² + β² = (α + β)² - 2αβ
• Difference of Roots: |α - β| = √D / |a|
• Symmetric Fraction: α/β + β/α = (α² + β²) / (αβ)
💡 Worked Test Problem
For what value of k will the equation 3x² - 12x + k = 0 have equal roots?
Condition: Equal roots occur when discriminant D = b² - 4ac = 0.
Calculation: (-12)² - 4(3)(k) = 0 ⟹ 144 - 12k = 0 ⟹ 12k = 144 ⟹ k = 12.
Result: k = 12.

Set Theory & Venn Diagrams

Round 1, 3, 4, 5 Syllabus • Cardinality • De Morgan's Laws
▼
Key Cardinality Rule: When combining sets, elements belonging to both sets must not be double-counted. Subsets counting formulas appear frequently in collegiate competition rounds.
Two-Set Overlap & Region Decomposition
Cardinality & Set Identities
Inclusion-Exclusion Principle (2 & 3 Sets)
n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A ∩ B)
• Disjoint Sets: If A ∩ B = ∅, then n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B).
• Neither Set: n((A ∪ B)') = n(U) - n(A ∪ B).
• Exactly One Set: n(A \ B) + n(B \ A) = n(A ∪ B) - n(A ∩ B).
• Three Sets: n(A∪B∪C) = Σn(A) - Σn(A∩B) + n(A∩B∩C).
Subsets & Power Set Counting
Total Subsets = 2ⁿ | Proper Subsets = 2ⁿ - 1
• A set with 5 elements has 2⁵ = 32 subsets.
• Proper subsets exclude the set itself: 32 - 1 = 31.
• Non-empty proper subsets exclude both ∅ and the set itself: 32 - 2 = 30.
De Morgan's Laws
(A ∪ B)′ = A′ ∩ B′ | (A ∩ B)′ = A′ ∪ B′
Complement of union is intersection of complements; complement of intersection is union of complements.
💡 Worked Test Problem
In a group of 50 students, 30 like Cricket, 25 like Football, and 10 like both. How many like neither?
Step 1 (Union): n(C ∪ F) = n(C) + n(F) - n(C ∩ F) = 30 + 25 - 10 = 45.
Step 2 (Neither): n((C ∪ F)') = Total - n(C ∪ F) = 50 - 45 = 5.
Result: Exactly 5 students like neither sport.

Averages, Percentages & Series Sums

Round 2 & Round 3 Syllabus • Harmonic Speed • Series Formulas
▼
Key Concept: Consecutive sequences and speed problems have instant mathematical shortcuts that avoid lengthy brute-force calculations.
Averages & Shortcuts
Round-Trip Average Speed (Harmonic Mean)
Average Speed = (2 × x × y) / (x + y)
Applicable whenever equal distances are traveled at speed x and return at speed y. (Example: 40 km/h out and 60 km/h back gives (2×40×60)/100 = 48 km/h, NOT 50 km/h!).
Average Weight / Age Replacement Formula
New Person = Excluded Person + (Total Members × Change in Avg)
If 15 students average 40 kg, and adding a teacher raises average by 1 kg: New Total = 16 × 41 = 656; Teacher = 656 - 600 = 56 kg.
Percentages & Expenditure Rules
Price vs. Consumption Invariance
Consumption Reduction = [r / (100 + r)] × 100%
If price rises by 25%, consumption reduction = [25 / 125] × 100% = 20%.
If A's salary is 20% less than B's, B is [20 / 80] × 100% = 25% higher than A.
Successive Percentage Change
Net Change % = x + y + (x × y) / 100
When increased by x% and then decreased by x%, the net result is ALWAYS a decrease of (x / 10)² %. (Example: +20% and -20% = -4% net).
Natural Series Summation Shortcuts
Sum of Series Formulas
Σn = n(n+1)/2 | Σ(First n Odd) = n² | Σ(First n Even) = n(n+1)
• Sum of squares: Σn² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6
• Sum of cubes: Σn³ = [n(n + 1) / 2]²

Exponents, Powers & Indices

Round 3 Syllabus • Laws of Indices • Base Equating Rules
▼
Base Equating Rule: If a^x = a^y (with a > 0, a ≠ 1), then the powers must be strictly equal: x = y. Express both sides with the smallest prime base (2, 3, 5).
Laws of Indices
Master Rules of Exponents
aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ | aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ | (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
• Negative Exponent: a⁻ⁿ = 1 / aⁿ and (a/b)⁻ⁿ = (b/a)ⁿ
• Fractional Exponent: a^(m/n) = ⁿ√(aᵐ) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ
• Zero Power: a⁰ = 1 (for any a ≠ 0)
• Comparing Powers: Reduce exponents by dividing by their HCF.
💡 Worked Test Problem
Solve for x: 2^(x + 3) + 2^x = 72
Step 1: Factor out common 2^x: 2^x(2³ + 1) = 72.
Step 2: Simplify brackets: 2^x(8 + 1) = 72 ⟹ 2^x(9) = 72.
Step 3: Divide: 2^x = 8 = 2³.
Result: x = 3.

Profit, Loss, Marked Price & Discount

Round 3 & Round 5 Syllabus • Successive Discounts • Equal SP Rule
▼
The Golden Chain: Cost Price (CP) is the foundation for Profit/Loss %. Marked Price (MP) is the foundation for Discount %.
Commercial Transaction Transformation Chain
Core Commercial Formulas
Profit, Loss & Selling Price
Profit % = [(SP - CP) / CP] × 100% | Loss % = [(CP - SP) / CP] × 100%
• SP = CP × (100 ± Gain/Loss %) / 100
• CP = SP × 100 / (100 ± Gain/Loss %)
Successive Discounts Formula
Net Discount % = d₁ + d₂ - (d₁ × d₂) / 100
• Buy X, Get Y Free: Effective Discount % = [Y / (X + Y)] × 100%. Example: Buy 3 Get 1 Free = 1/4 × 100 = 25%.
• Equal SP Rule: If two items are sold at the same price, one at x% profit and the other at x% loss, there is ALWAYS an overall loss of (x / 10)² %.
Dishonest Dealer / False Weights
Gain % = [Error / (True Value - Error)] × 100%
Example: Using 950g instead of 1000g: Error = 50g. Gain % = (50 / 950) × 100 = 5.26%.

Pythagorean Identities & High-Speed Shortcuts
Fundamental Identities
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 | 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ | 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
• Conjugate Pairs: If sec θ + tan θ = k, then sec θ - tan θ = 1/k.
• Complementary Angles: When A + B = 90°, sin A = cos B and sin² A + sin² B = 1.
• Product Chain: cos 0° × cos 1° × ... × cos 90° = 0 (since cos 90° = 0).

Mathematical Reasoning: Clocks & Calendar

Round 2, 3, 4, 5 Syllabus • Hand Angles • Leap Year & Odd Days
▼
Clock Face Angle Arc (At 3:40)
Clocks Formulas
Angle Between Clock Hands
Angle θ = |30 × H - 5.5 × M|
• Reflex Angle: If θ > 180°, reflex angle = 360° - θ.
• Coincidence (Together): M = (60/11) × H minutes past H.
• Straight Line (Opposite 180°): M = (60/11) × (H ± 6).
• Hands coincide exactly 22 times in 24 hours.
Calendar & Odd Days Calculation
Odd Days & Leap Year Rules
Ordinary Year = 1 Odd Day | Leap Year = 2 Odd Days
• Odd Days Calculation: Total Days mod 7 = Odd Days.
• Century Rule: A century year is a leap year ONLY if divisible by 400 (e.g. 2000 was a leap year; 1900, 2100 are ordinary years).
• Day Shift: In an ordinary year, 1st Jan and 31st Dec are the same day of the week. In a leap year, 31st Dec is one day ahead.
Polygon Diagonals Shortcut
Number of Diagonals = n(n - 3) / 2
For an 8-sided polygon (octagon): 8(8 - 3) / 2 = 8 × 5 / 2 = 20 diagonals.
